## 1. Purpose

Fernhill Bank resolves errors in electronic fund transfers promptly, fairly and within the time limits of Regulation E. This policy states what the bank commits to and who is accountable.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

- Every consumer account at Fernhill Bank that can send or receive electronic fund transfers.
- Every employee and contractor who receives, investigates or decides a consumer's notice of error.

This policy does not apply to business accounts or to wire transfers.

## 3. Definitions

**Electronic fund transfer (EFT).** A transfer of funds started through an electronic terminal, telephone, computer or magnetic tape that debits or credits a consumer's account. Examples are debit card purchases, ATM transactions and direct deposits.

**Error.** Any of the following (12 CFR 1005.11(a)(1)):

- An unauthorized EFT.
- An incorrect EFT to or from the consumer's account.
- An EFT missing from a periodic statement.
- A computational or bookkeeping error by the bank that relates to an EFT.
- The consumer receiving an incorrect amount of money from an ATM.
- An EFT that is not identified on a receipt or statement as Regulation E requires.
- The consumer's request for documentation that Regulation E requires, or for more information about an EFT.

A routine balance inquiry, a request for tax or recordkeeping information, and a request for duplicate copies are not errors (12 CFR 1005.11(a)(2)).

**Notice of error.** A consumer's oral or written statement that an error occurred, which meets section 4.1.

**Business day.** Any day on which the bank's offices are open to the public for substantially all business functions (12 CFR 1005.2(d)).

**Provisional credit.** An amount the bank credits to the consumer's account while an investigation continues.

## 4. Policy statements

### 4.1 Accepting a notice of error

The bank accepts a notice of error by telephone, in a branch, by secure message or by mail. The bank treats a notice as valid when it:

- Is received no later than 60 days after the bank sent the periodic statement on which the error first appeared.
- Lets the bank identify the consumer's name and account number.
- Says why the consumer believes there is an error and, as far as possible, gives the type, date and amount.

No employee may refuse or delay a notice because the consumer has not first contacted a merchant.

### 4.2 Written confirmation

The bank may ask a consumer to confirm an oral notice in writing within 10 business days. When it does, the employee tells the consumer about the requirement and gives the address during the same contact.

The bank starts its investigation when it receives the oral notice. It does not wait for the written confirmation.

### 4.3 Standard time limit

The bank investigates promptly and decides whether an error occurred within 10 business days of receiving the notice of error.

### 4.4 Extended time limit and provisional credit

When the bank cannot decide within 10 business days, it may take up to 45 days from the date it received the notice, on these conditions:

- The bank provisionally credits the consumer's account for the amount of the alleged error, with interest where it applies, within 10 business days of receiving the notice.
- The bank tells the consumer the amount and date of the credit within 2 business days of making it.
- The consumer has full use of the credited funds during the investigation.

**Bank standard.** Regulation E lets an institution withhold up to $50 from a provisional credit in some cases of unauthorized transfer. Fernhill Bank credits the full amount. Regulation E also allows an institution to skip provisional credit when it asked for written confirmation and did not receive it within 10 business days. The Dispute Operations Manager decides whether to rely on that exception and records the reason.

### 4.5 Longer limits for certain transfers

The following limits replace those in 4.3 and 4.4:

| Situation | Decision limit without provisional credit | Limit with provisional credit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The transfer took place within 30 days after the first deposit to the account | 20 business days | 90 days |
| The transfer resulted from a point-of-sale debit card transaction | 10 business days | 90 days |
| The transfer was not initiated within a state | 10 business days | 90 days |

### 4.6 Correcting errors and reporting results

When the bank determines that an error occurred, it corrects the error within 1 business day. A correction includes any interest and any fees the bank charged because of the error.

The bank reports the results to the consumer within 3 business days of completing its investigation.

### 4.7 When no error is found

When the bank determines that no error occurred, or that a different error occurred, it sends the consumer a written explanation of its findings. The explanation tells the consumer that they may ask for copies of the documents the bank relied on. The bank provides those copies promptly on request.

Before the bank debits a provisional credit, it notifies the consumer of the date and amount of the debit. For 5 business days after that notice, the bank honors checks, drafts and preauthorized transfers from the account that it would have paid had the credit remained, and it does not charge overdraft fees as a result.

### 4.8 Disclosures

The bank gives each consumer an error resolution notice when the account is opened and at least once each calendar year. The notice is substantially similar to Model Form A-3 in Appendix A to Regulation E. Deposit Operations owns delivery of the notice.

### 4.9 Records

The bank keeps evidence of compliance with this policy for at least 2 years from the date an action was required. Records for a matter under investigation or legal action are kept until the matter is closed.

## 5. Roles and responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| **Compliance Committee** | Approves this policy and each change to it. |
| **Chief Compliance Officer** | Owns this policy. Reports exceptions to the committee each quarter. |
| **Dispute Operations Manager** | Owns the procedure. Makes sure each claim meets its deadlines. Approves use of the extended time limit. |
| **Dispute Operations Analyst** | Receives, investigates and decides claims under the procedure. |
| **Front-line staff** | Take a notice of error from the consumer and log it the same day. |
| **Internal Audit** | Tests a sample of closed claims against this policy each year. |

## 6. Exceptions

No exception may extend a time limit set by Regulation E. Any other exception to this policy needs written approval from the Chief Compliance Officer before the fact.
